# T40 — Podcasts from YouTube Music

Spike date: 2026-11-28. Spike code was kept out of the repository and is not merged.

## Question

Before the podcast contracts (T41) are written (design: [`../podcasts.md`](../podcasts.md)):

1. Which `ytmusicapi` calls work without an account for podcast search, the show page, the episode list and episode details? What shapes, fields and paging do they have, and where do chapters and descriptions come from?
2. What does `yt-dlp` offer for episodes: formats, sizes, download time?
3. Can a long episode that is not downloaded yet be streamed and sought with the current streaming-while-downloading design (T03a, T16)?

## Short answers

1. **Yes, without an account:** search, show page, episode list and episode details all work. One real problem: in show and episode pages the `date` field that `ytmusicapi` returns is the **view count**, not the date.
2. Episodes are ordinary videos: Opus/WebM `251` (about 105 to 145 kbit/s) and M4A `140`. A 73-minute episode is 57 MB and took 13 s. **The current limits refuse most episodes** (30 minutes by default).
3. **Yes, with no new container.** The two real `251` files have their index at the front. Seeking to minute 40 of an episode that was still downloading gave audio after 4 s at 8 MB/s and 16 s at 2 MB/s. At 1 MB/s or less the wait is longer than the app's 25 s read timeout.

## Setup and limits of this spike

- `ytmusicapi` 1.12.4 (the locked version), anonymous, `language="en"`, through the server's own `YouTubeSession`. `location` was left at its default. About 60 requests, no `429`, no bot wall.
- `yt-dlp` 2026.10.26 (a local install, not the image's zipapp) with `yt-dlp-ejs` 0.8.0 and Deno 2.9.7, with the server's own command line (`worker/ytdlp.py::build_args`) or a metadata-only variant of it. No cookies, no PO token provider.
- `ffmpeg`/`ffprobe` 9.0.2.
- **A home connection, not the production server.** Nothing here says how a server's address is treated (bot wall, throttling).
- Two episodes were downloaded and deleted afterwards: *Linux Inlaws* S01E62 (34 min) and S01E61 (73 min), published on Hacker Public Radio under CC BY-SA 4.0 (checked on the HPR episode pages; YouTube itself reports no licence for them).

## 1. `ytmusicapi` without an account

| Call | Result | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `search(q, filter="podcasts")` | works, 20 by default; `limit=50` returned 60 | 1 request per 20, 70 to 570 KiB |
| `search(q, filter="episodes")` | works | the same |
| `search(q)` unfiltered | mixes `podcast` and `episode` results with music | 1 request |
| `get_podcast(id, limit=N)` | works | 1 request for the newest 20, then 1 per 100 |
| `get_episode(videoId)` | works | 1 request, about 30 KiB |
| `get_song(videoId)` | works: exact length and publication time | 1 `player` request, about 465 KiB |
| `get_channel(id)` / `get_channel_episodes` | works (10 episodes, then 180 with `params`); not needed | 0.6 to 4.5 MB |
| `get_episodes_playlist("RDPN")` ("New Episodes") | **fails** without an account (`KeyError`) | — |

Calls took 0.07 to 1.2 s. Each new `YTMusic` object also loads the home page once (about 650 KiB).

### Shapes

**Show search hit:** `resultType`, `title`, `browseId` (`MPSP` + a playlist id starting `PL` or `OL`), `thumbnails` (60 to 544 px). **No author**, although the raw response has it in the second column: the parser drops it.

**Episode search hit:** `title`, `videoId`, `videoType` (`MUSIC_VIDEO_TYPE_PODCAST_EPISODE`), `date` (correct here: `"Nov 15"`, `"Oct 30, 2024"`), `podcast: {id, name}`, `isAvailable`, `live`, `thumbnails`. **No duration, no description.**

**`get_podcast`:** `title`, `author: {name, id}` (the channel), `description`, `thumbnails`, `saved` (meaningless without an account), `episodes[]`. Each episode: `videoId`, `browseId` (`MPED` + video id), `title`, `description`, `duration`, `date`, `thumbnails`, `videoType`, `index` (always `null` here).

**`get_episode`:** `title`, `author: {name, id}` (here the **show**), `playlistId` (the show), `date`, `duration`, `thumbnails`, `description` (a list of text, link and timestamp elements, or `None`). `saved` and `progressPercentage` are meaningless without an account.

**`get_song`:** `videoDetails.lengthSeconds`, `microformat.microformatDataRenderer.publishDate` (ISO 8601 with offset), `playabilityStatus`.

Thumbnails came only from `i.ytimg.com`, `yt3.googleusercontent.com`, `yt3.ggpht.com` and `lh3.googleusercontent.com`: all on the approved list.

### Dates and durations

- **`date` is wrong in `get_podcast`, `get_episode` and the channel lists.** It holds `"1.2K views"`. The raw subtitle is `[views, " • ", date]` and the parser takes the first part. This happened for every episode of every show probed.
- The real date in the raw response is a display string: `"4d ago"`, `"Jun 28"` (current year, no year) or `"Mar 6, 2024"`. At best it is precise to the day.
- An exact time needs `get_song` (one request per episode) or the download (`yt-dlp` `timestamp`).
- `duration` is text: `"58 min"`, `"1 hr"`, `"1 hr 16 min"`, `"13 min 32 sec"`. Usually only minute precision.
- The lists were newest first. A show that YouTube orders oldest first was not looked for.

### Paging

- `get_podcast(limit=N)`: `limit` is a minimum. The first page has 20 episodes, each continuation 100, so `limit=100` returned 120.
- `limit=None` on a 579-episode show returned all 579, no duplicates, in 7 requests (about 9.8 MB of raw JSON).
- No cursor is exposed: a later page cannot be asked for on its own. The server should page from its own database.
- A refresh with `limit=20` is exactly one request (about 450 KiB).

### Errors

- Unknown episode: HTTP 404 (`get_episode`).
- Unknown show: a `KeyError` from inside the parser, which `map_error(lookup=True)` already turns into `CatalogNotFound`.

### Descriptions and chapters

- Episode rows of `get_podcast` already carry the **full description as plain text** with line breaks (same length as `get_episode` for the three compared). `get_episode` is only needed for link targets.
- There is no chapter field. `get_episode` marks timestamps in the description (`seconds` is `None` for `0:00`), with the chapter name in the text that follows. `yt-dlp` has a `chapters` list: 8 entries for one episode whose description has 9 timed lines (the last one, at the very end, was dropped).
- Many episodes have no timestamps at all.
- Parsing the plain description on the server (as the design says) needs no extra request and covers both sources.

## 2. `yt-dlp` on episodes

### Formats and sizes

| Episode | `251` Opus/WebM | `140` AAC/M4A |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 34 min | 27.3 MB, 106 kbit/s | 33.3 MB, 129 kbit/s |
| 73 min | 57.4 MB, 104 kbit/s | 71.2 MB, 129 kbit/s |
| 96 min (metadata only) | 102.4 MB, 143 kbit/s | 92.8 MB, 129 kbit/s |

- All have an exact `filesize` before the download, so the api can announce `Content-Length`.
- One episode had **about 20 auto-dubbed audio tracks** (`251-0` … `251-19`). The server's format selector picked `251-19`, "English (US) original (default)", so `yt-dlp`'s language preference does the right thing. A test with a stored format list should pin this.

### Download time

The server's command line, `default` player client, home connection:

| Episode | First progress | Whole file | Rate after the first progress |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 34 min, 27.3 MB | 3.0 s | 6.4 s | 8.0 MB/s |
| 73 min, 57.4 MB | 3.8 s | 13.0 s | 6.2 MB/s |

### Limits that block episodes

- `UT_MAX_TRACK_DURATION_S` defaults to **1800 s** (maximum 6 h). The 34-minute episode was skipped with exit 0 and no file; both downloads needed the limit raised.
- `UT_MAX_DOWNLOAD_BYTES` (200 MiB) holds about 3.3 h at 143 kbit/s.
- Episodes need their own two limits.

### Player clients (not podcast-specific)

Both of these also happened with a music track (metadata only):

- The server's first ladder step, `web_safari,tv`, failed with `The page needs to be reloaded` (no PO token provider here). The ladder then uses `default`, which worked; the failed step costs about 2.4 s.
- `default` warned `n challenge solving failed` (the Deno solver raised `a.addOnDisposeCallback is not a function`). The formats were still listed and downloaded at full speed.

Neither was checked in the server image or against production. They are worth checking on their own.

## 3. Long episodes while downloading

### Layout of the real files

Both downloaded `251` files are laid out as `EBML, SeekHead, Info, Tracks, Cues, Clusters`. The Cues are at the front: 3.6 KB (34 min) and 7.8 KB (73 min), before byte 8 100. `ffprobe` read the full duration from the first 2 MB. This also confirms, on real files, what T03a could only assume for `251`.

### Seek and resume

The 73-minute file was fed to the server's own streaming code (`plan_start`, `respond`, `step`, `JobWatch`, `TailResponse`) by a fake writer at a fixed rate. `ffmpeg` then started at minute 40 with a 25 s read timeout. It asked for `bytes=0-`, then `bytes=31127268-`, both answered `206` at once.

| Download rate | Audio at minute 40 after | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8 MB/s (about what was measured) | 4.2 s | |
| 2 MB/s | 15.8 s | |
| 1 MB/s | 31.6 s | one read timed out first |
| 0.25 MB/s | never (gave up at 50.7 s) | |

Add the 3 to 4 s `yt-dlp` needs before the first byte (more if the first client fails). **No change to the state machine is needed:** a range ahead of the writer waits for it.

The limit is the client's patience. The app's read timeout is 25 s (`CachePolicy.READ_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`). `ffmpeg` is not Media3: **how Media3 retries here, and how long it lasts before failing, was not measured.**

### Options

| Option | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| Wait for the whole download | Simple, but the first sound waits 13 s or more for a 73-minute episode even on a fast link. Only for `140`, as today. |
| Stream while downloading (current design) | Works for `251`. Recommended. |
| Download ahead | Recommended as well: followed shows with automatic download, and "Continue listening" episodes that were freed. |
| Another container | Not needed. A remux would also change the bytes. |

Other points:

- `140` is still marked not progressive (`CONTAINERS`), so an episode without `251` plays only once finished. No such episode was seen.
- The worker runs **one job at a time**. An episode being downloaded delays a music stream request queued behind it (about 13 s for 73 minutes here; minutes on a slow link or a 4-hour episode).
- Episodes are not tagged or remuxed, so **the archived file can be the downloaded bytes themselves**. Then Range requests and the client's cache stay valid across the end of the download, unlike music.

## Not verified

- Anything from the production server or a data-centre address; behaviour with cookies or a PO token provider; the image's `yt-dlp` zipapp.
- Media3 on a real episode file, on a device: seek, resume, retries after the read timeout.
- A download slower than the link allowed, throttling over long sessions, episodes over 96 minutes, 4-hour files.
- Episodes that are members-only, age-gated, live, premiering, region-blocked or removed; `isAvailable: false` results.
- Shows ordered oldest first; shows without view counts (where `date` might be right); other `location` values; non-English shows' text fields.
- How long the podcast paging stays stable: only one long show was paged to the end.
- Third-party OpenSubsonic clients with podcasts, or with a WebM episode.

## Recommendation

Go ahead with phase 1 as designed. No new dependency and no new host.

1. Catalog: `search` (two filters), `get_podcast` (`limit=20` to refresh), `get_episode` only for link targets, `get_song` for **new** episodes only (exact date and length). Do not use channel calls or `RDPN`.
2. Never trust `ytmusicapi`'s `date` from show or episode pages. Keep the upstream order; leave `publishedAt` empty until `get_song` or the download gives it. If day-level dates are wanted for a whole back catalogue, the server needs its own parser for the subtitle.
3. Keep streaming while downloading, with the downloaded `251` file as the archived file.
4. Download ahead what the user is likely to play; in the app (T47) allow a longer wait for episodes than 25 s, and show that the episode is being fetched.
5. Separate duration and size limits for episodes.

### Proposed SPEC §11 decision text

> - **Podcasts from YouTube Music** (2026-11-28, T40):
>   - Anonymous `ytmusicapi` is enough: search, `get_podcast`, `get_episode`, and `get_song` for the exact date and length of new episodes. Its `date` on show and episode pages is the view count and is ignored.
>   - Episodes stream while downloading with the current design (Opus/WebM `251` has its index at the front, checked on real files). No remux, no tags: the downloaded file is the archived file.
>   - A seek or resume far ahead waits for the download; the server downloads ahead where it can, and the app allows a longer wait for episodes.
>   - Episodes have their own duration and size limits.

## Contract implications for T41

**Ids** (`contracts/ids.md`)

- UUIDv7 rows for shows and episodes.
- Virtual ids of their own, so an episode never materializes a track: for example `ytpodcast:<playlistId>` (store it without the `MPSP` prefix; `^[A-Za-z0-9_-]{1,64}$`; the longest seen with the prefix was 38) and `ytepisode:<videoId>` (`^[A-Za-z0-9_-]{11}$`).

**Database**

- Tables for shows, episodes, follows and progress. Episodes keep the upstream position, a nullable `publishedAt`, a nullable duration and the plain description.
- `jobs` cannot hold episodes as it is: `kind` is `CHECK (kind IN ('download'))` and `track_id` is `NOT NULL REFERENCES tracks`. It needs a rebuilt table (new table, copy, rename, which `contracts/db/README.md` leaves to the coordinator) or a second job table. One table keeps one queue, one lease logic and the streaming code unchanged.

**Internal worker API**

- A new job kind (for example `episode`) in the claim's `kinds`. The default stays `[download]`, so an older worker never gets one.
- Such a job has an episode id instead of `trackId`, no music hints, and its own duration and size limits.
- Finishing it must not require tags or ReplayGain: place the downloaded file as it is, and report size, content type, duration and, if wanted, publication time and chapters.

**Queue**

- Episode stream requests at the stream priority; automatic downloads below imports. Decide whether one sequential worker is enough.

**`ut-v1`**

- Show hit: id, title, cover; **author optional** (needs our own parsing). Episode hit: id, title, show, cover; no duration; date optional.
- Episode lists paged from the server's database.
- Episode detail: description as plain text, chapters `[{startMs, title}]`, `publishedAt` and `durationMs` nullable, state `remote | downloading | ready | failed`, and download progress (bytes and size) for the app's waiting screen.
- A follow fetches the newest page; the back catalogue is fetched on demand, with a cap.

**OpenSubsonic**

- `stream` must accept an episode that is still `remote` (it starts the download, like a song). Decide how `remote` maps to the Subsonic episode `status` and whether `streamId` is given for it.

**Errors**

- An episode over the limits needs a clear, non-retryable answer (today it surfaces as `video_unavailable`).

**Fake catalog and examples**

- Show search, episode search, show page, episode and episode info, with invented data in the observed shapes, including: no author on a show hit, no duration on an episode hit, a missing publication date, a description with timestamps, and several audio languages in a format list.

**Configuration**

- Episode duration and size limits. `CONTAINERS` stays as it is.
